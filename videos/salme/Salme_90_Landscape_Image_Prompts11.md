## Visual direction

Use the references for bold contours and simple characters, then build depth with the setting. Draw rounded faces with small eyes, clear brows and slim dark limbs. Block clothes into one or two colours and give each person a readable gesture. Keep foreground objects crisp and large. Let timber, earth and water carry a little painted texture, with softer shapes in the distance. Use one clear light source and broad pools of colour.

## Assembly

1. Copy one prompt block per image. Attach one or two of the style frames above if your generator accepts references. When a prompt names a previous shot, use that generated image to match its character, object or framing. Start with images 001, 006, 007 and 024 to establish recurring designs.
2. Generate in **16:9** and compose for a phone. Keep faces and clues inside the central area. Reserve a quiet band around 72–82% of the frame height for captions and keep the right edge clear of crucial detail. Add captions in the editor.
3. Place the ElevenLabs narration on the timeline first. Each time range is an editing target. Align the cut to its quoted **Voice cue** once the voiceover is recorded, and use the **Edit** note for a crop, hold or transition. A small move within a still can carry a second beat without another illustration.
4. Typeset the dates, counts, labels and routes by hand. The atlas prompts create plates for verified coastlines and paths. Treat the burial layouts as schematics and label historical reconstructions. Keep the competing Salme explanations visibly marked as possibilities.
